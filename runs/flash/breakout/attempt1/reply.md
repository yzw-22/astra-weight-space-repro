Design: I hand-code a tracking controller. The first hidden layer computes approximations of paddle-minus-ball column, horizontal direction (with special handling at the side walls), vertical downward direction, and one-hot indicators for ball rows 3 through 7. The second hidden layer forms conjunctions “row = r AND ball descending AND moving right/left”; these conjunctions encode the projected bottom intercept column `ball_x + dx*(8-row)`. The output layer compares this projected intercept with the paddle position: if the paddle is left of the target it drives action 3, if right it drives action 1, and if close it chooses no-op. While the ball is rising, no projection is applied, so the paddle follows the ball’s current column; this leaves it one step behind in the direction of motion, exactly enough to reach the projected landing spot after the ball turns downward. Large negative biases suppress up/down/fire.
